---
title: "Score outcomes"
description: "The persona's words are perception, not a verdict. Judge task success from your own environment's state."
mode: "how-to"
---

The persona tells you how a moment felt: a `comment`, a `sentiment`, a `status`. That is its **perception**, not a judgment of whether the task actually succeeded. A persona can be confidently wrong, exactly as a real person can. If you score success off its words, you will record passes on failed goals.

## The persona can be confidently wrong

In a real run where a volume control had been secretly reversed, the persona declared "Volume 10 is perfect!" and finished with a `completed` status, while the frame plainly read **VOL 12**. Scoring off `session_status` and `sentiment` would have logged a clean pass on a broken control.

This is not a defect in the persona; it is the point. The persona is the **experience layer**: did the interface feel confusing, where did it hesitate, what did it misread. A real person would have made the same mistake in front of the same reversed control. What the persona cannot be is the **verdict layer**, the source of truth on whether the goal was met.

## Score from your own state

Judge task outcomes from your environment's own state, the ground truth you already hold, and never from the persona's narration.

1. Keep a source of truth in your environment: the value the control was set to, the page the flow reached, the order that was placed.
2. After the run, assert against that state on your side. Did the volume reach the target? Did the checkout complete?
3. Use the persona's `comment`, `sentiment`, and `felt_intensity` for the experience read: where it hesitated, what confused it, where friction showed up.

The two layers answer different questions. Your state answers "did it work". The persona answers "how did it feel to try". You want both, kept separate.

## Never feed your state to the persona

If your web adapter exposes a `state()` method (see [author your environment](/api/guides/environment-authoring#the-web-adapter)), it is for your own scoring only. **Never send that state to the persona as an observation.** The persona perceives only rendered senses; handing it the true state turns it from a simulated human into a solver and destroys the very signal you are running it for. Keep `state()` on your side of the wire, and assert against it there.

## In intent mode, no_match is its own signal

A `no_match` resolution is not a failure to score against your state; it is a finding in its own right. The persona reached for something your environment does not offer, and `unmet_expectation` names the gap. Read those alongside your state assertions: your state tells you whether the goal was met, and the `no_match` stream tells you where the persona expected an affordance that was not there. See [intent and resolution](/api/concepts/intent-and-resolution#the-finding-channel).
